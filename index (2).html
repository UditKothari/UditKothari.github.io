RMIT EV Living Lab · Semester 1 team results

Testing a grid-forming battery

We built and tested a detailed electrical model of a solar and EV microgrid. It gives us a way to check future dispatch decisions against the inverter response they produce.

The research gap

In our literature review, several learning-based dispatch studies tested decisions on simplified energy models. I wanted to know whether those same decisions would hold up in a detailed electrical model where the battery inverter has to respond to changes in load and keep the microgrid stable.

My contribution

I wrote part of the engineering methodology and shared responsibility for revising the Simulink/Simscape Electrical model and running feasibility tests. I also documented the model iterations with a teammate. Our team moved from a reference model to an Artemis-compatible testbench so we could work towards real-time testing on OPAL-RT.

We started with load steps, then tested changing sunlight, excess solar and a daily profile with EV charging. Working through those cases made it clear that when the microgrid disconnects from the grid, the battery already has a job: keeping voltage and frequency steady. That changed what we plan to let the learning controller adjust in islanded operation.

What the simulations showed

  1. Changes in demandAt the 60 kW load command, the battery supplied 59.7 kW and the network bus settled at 0.989 pu, or 98.9% of nominal voltage.
  2. Surplus solarThe original voltage loop developed an 11.4 Hz oscillation while charging. After retuning the loop, the model absorbed 6.38 kW of surplus solar at 50.000 Hz. The team repeated the 60 kW discharge test and found no new growing oscillation.
  3. Daily profileFrequency stayed between 49.92 and 50.06 Hz during the compressed solar and EV demand profile. This was a dynamics test with a compressed time scale.

Residential solar and storage

I used Melbourne smart-meter data to compare PV, battery and generator configurations under flat and time-of-use tariffs. A grid-connected PV-only run with an 11.6 kW array modelled a net present cost of A$31,910.71 and an energy cost of A$0.08249/kWh. I assessed outage performance separately when comparing designs with backup supply.

RMIT FMM Lab · Research internship

Wearable biosensor hardware

I designed a flexible circuit board for a glucose and sodium sensor that had to fit inside the arm of a pair of glasses.

Learning the sensing problem

Biochemistry was new to me. I learnt enough about how each sensor produced a signal and how the two could interfere to make informed circuit and layout decisions. I worked between my biochemistry and electrical supervisors, checking what the sensing sites needed with one and what the circuit could reliably deliver with the other.

Going back to the circuit

I used op-amp fundamentals to work through how to condition the small sensor signals, then checked the power supply around the Bluetooth bursts. My calculations predicted a 600 mV supply dip with the original coin cell, so I moved to a LiPo and gave the analogue and digital sections their own regulators. Diffusion calculations led me to separate the two sensing sites on a T-shaped flex with an estimated diffusion time of more than 90 seconds between sites. The revised board fit the 12 mm width constraint.

What changed between revisions

Sensor interference
Separated the sensing sites on the flex after checking diffusion.
Supply dip
Reworked the battery and regulator layout around the Bluetooth load.
Parts went out of stock
Checked replacement parts against the original circuit requirements and repeated the relevant simulations before updating the layout.

Going through several revisions taught me where to spend time. I checked the calculation or simulation most likely to force a redesign before committing hours to layout. I took the revised flex PCB through fabrication.

PLC sorting station

I programmed a live sorting station in ladder logic. Limit switches, light barriers, colour sensing and conveyor feedback drove the step sequencer from processing through colour sorting to three output lanes.

EV detection from meter data

I built classifiers to infer EV ownership from 48-interval household load profiles. I looked at overnight ramps and stretches of high demand, then checked precision and recall on held-out households to see how often other appliances were mistaken for charging.
